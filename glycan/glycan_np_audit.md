# Generative models for glycans and microbial natural products: evidence audit

Audit date: **23 October 2026**. Scope: research from 2022 through the audit date, with the explicitly requested SweetTalk/SweetNet historical precedents. This is a documented literature search, not a claim to enumerate every molecular generator ever applied to a natural compound. General protein/AMP generators, ordinary virtual screening, and general molecular models without an explicit natural-product experiment are outside the main inventory. Preprints are identified as such.

**Decision:** choose a small, constrained glycan-topology design experiment if you prefer the less crowded domain. However, **“glycan generation does not exist” is false**. GlycoBART generates glycan sequences from spectra; a 2024 Boltzmann/Markov system infers structures from lectin fingerprints; GlycanGT completes masked structures; historical SweetTalk already searches glycan mutations using a property predictor. A defensible gap concerns *functional topology design with structural-family splits, selectivity conditions, and a serious near-duplicate/surrogate-exploitation audit*. Experimental function remains unproved by an in-silico project.

**Strict feasibility finding:** no complete neural-generator reproduction in this audit has been verified to satisfy **all** of immediate openly licensed data, one free T4/P100 with 12–16 GB, training **plus evaluation under four GPU-hours**, and a fresh environment ready in five minutes. Reduced training, using released weights, and reproducing the entire published experiment are different claims. Several reduced experiments look plausible; none should be called a measured Colab pass.

## Evidence notation and interpretation

- **C**: confirmed at a primary source: paper, supplement, official repository/config, dataset metadata/API, or downloadable artifact. For a paper, this confirms what the authors report; it does not independently validate their experiment.
- **D**: calculated from confirmed artifacts, such as counting CSV rows or multiplying epochs by batches. It is not an author-reported number.
- **E**: explicitly estimated here. Throughput assumptions are unmeasured; estimates are planning ranges, not benchmarks or confidence intervals.
- **NR**: not reported in the inspected sources, or not independently established as specified. “License NR” does not mean forbidden; it means the strict open-license requirement is **not demonstrated**.
- **Immediate** means no application/login was needed for the source or listed artifact checked. Metadata/file-list access was checked for large archives; not every multi-gigabyte artifact was fully downloaded. Mutable GitHub/default-branch and dataset pages were inspected at the audit date.

GPU-hours = number of actively used GPUs × elapsed hours. A job's requested time limit is not its elapsed time. Epoch counts in filenames can instead mean optimizer steps. A multi-GPU hardware statement alone cannot establish runtime or single-GPU impossibility. Unless stated otherwise below, **wall-clock and GPU-hours are NR**, and the estimate is for a T4, not a measured conversion from the authors' hardware.

Repository code licenses, weight licenses, dataset licenses, and the rights in upstream third-party measurements are separate. An MIT repository does not automatically relicense every imported array measurement or commercial reference dataset. Current data counts are not substituted for a paper's historical snapshot.

**Compute audit detail.** GlycoBART source contains timing print statements, not a published timing log. Its Zenodo API explicitly identifies **CC BY4.0**; an unpopulated license section in a rendered page is not evidence of no license. GlycanGT's checked-in full recipe is unusually long: its large model alone would need **≤11.3 ms/update** to finish in four hours before evaluation; running all four sizes would need **≤4 ms/update** (D). Those are required-speed calculations, not benchmarks. A smaller checkpoint or a 10-epoch rerun should be described as a reduced experiment.

**Historical design precedent.** SweetTalk's mutation/ranking routine is enough to defeat a broad “first property-designed glycan” claim. G4 and G5 establish inverse *identification* from observed measurements, which is a different task from designing a molecule to achieve a requested binding profile. G7 recovers missing labels on supplied connectivity. G8 samples geometry while keeping chemical identity fixed. These distinctions are essential when defining novelty for a final project.

**Exact-reproduction discrepancies in the antibiotic papers.** Cell's paper counts do not match its released predictor CSVs or linked generator corpora. The released NG archive contains **50 checkpoints** and an explicit5fold×10ensemble config; the paper describes20-model final predictors. Linked F-VAE/hgraph ChEMBL text files contain **1,488,640 /1,799,433 rows**, versus the paper's1,686,695 (D). These are documented differences, not silently reconciled counts. Details and exact URLs are in [the antibiotic artifact audit](tasks/antibiotic_compute.md) and [machine-readable record](tasks/antibiotic_artifact_audit.json).

For JACS Au, the actual group is **Sieber/TUM**; reusing an ETH generator does not make it an ETH paper. The [official supplement](https://acs.figshare.com/articles/journal_contribution/30086237) is **5,419,172B,CC BY-NC4.0** (C metadata), pins generator commit `694824c8a6af0964461c7cc0846c0be994c4363e`, and supports the40epoch recipe and similarity analysis. Its default MEGx file is an evaluation reference. Public vendor-derived file availability is not proof of an explicit upstream open-data license.

**Compute estimates are intentionally rough.** For transformers, `6 × parameters × token presentations / effective FLOP/s` is a planning approximation that omits attention-length effects, padding, data loading and evaluation. For graph/LSTM models, `ceil(N/batch) × epochs × assumed seconds/update` is used instead. Repeating multiple seeds, hyperparameter search and generator sampling can exceed training itself. No T4 training was run during this literature audit.

## 4. Free automatic validity checks and measured runtime

**Natural products: use RDKit.** `Chem.MolFromSmiles` with sanitization checks parseability and chemical constraints including allowed valence, aromaticity/kekulization and ring handling; canonical isomeric SMILES helps exact deduplication. Enforce one connected component, allowed elements, charge and atom-count limits according to the project specification. These are user-defined filters in addition to the library's checks. RDKit can accept unstable, unsynthesizable or biologically inactive structures: **valid graph ≠ viable natural product ≠ antibiotic**. [Primary documentation](https://www.rdkit.org/docs/GettingStartedInPython.html).

**Glycans: use a glycan parser/tree check plus GlyLES→RDKit on the supported vocabulary.** Parse with [glycowork](https://github.com/BojarLab/glycowork); explicitly require a connected rooted tree, specified monomers/anomers/linkages and nonconflicting occupied linkage sites. GlyLES translates to atoms, allowing RDKit chemistry/canonicalization. The exact semantic/tree constraints must be implemented and tested for the chosen grammar: **GlyLES alone does not promise them**. Treat parse errors, ignored-modification warnings and unsupported tokens separately from chemical invalidity. Reject unsupported conversions rather than counting them as valid with missing chemistry. Neither tool checks lectin binding or biosynthesis. [GlyLES primary paper](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13321-023-00704-0).

This is a **local CPU benchmark, not a Colab GPU experiment**. GPU allocation can remain occupied during CPU evaluation, so this cannot simply be ignored in a four-hour Colab session.

**Novelty/memorization protocol for either domain:** canonicalize before splitting; report exact training membership, nearest-neighbor similarity distributions and a held-out structural-family test. For glycans, preserve branch/linkage/anomer identities and compare graph isomorphism plus tree/motif distances, rather than raw text. For NPs, retain a declared tautomer/stereo policy and use Morgan/Tanimoto nearest neighbors and scaffold overlap against **all available generator pretraining and fine-tuning data**, not merely the activity-labelled subset. Property improvement plus exact novelty is insufficient. Similarity cutoffs are analysis choices, not universal proof of new chemistry.

## 5. Which problems are already demonstrated, and which remain open?

“Demonstrated” below means shown on the paper's task/data, not universally solved biology.

### Which domain is more crowded with large labs?

**NP-like molecular/antibiotic generation is more crowded in this audit.** It includes A*STAR's library/discovery infrastructure, MIT/Broad/Wyss's experimentally validated antibiotic work, TUM's antibiotic synthesis pipeline, Zhejiang's broad generative frameworks, MIT's interaction-conditioned diffusion work and established chemical-LM groups. Competitors can combine pretraining, synthesis and substantial screening. This comparison follows the affiliations, experiments and compute reported above; **it is not a measured census of funding or all competing labs**. For example, WISE explicitly acknowledges A*STAR computing infrastructure and NRF/A*STAR funding. [Primary acknowledgements](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44259-026-00206-7).

Glycan topology/function design has fewer direct competitors in the audited set, with concentrated contributions from Bojar and collaborators, Nagoya, Buffalo, Peking and newer structural-model groups. It is **not compute-free**: GlycoBART,GlycanAA,GlycanGT and SweetFold have multi-GPU or lengthy recipes. The attractive low-budget opportunity is evaluation/constraints and a small model, rather than reproducing a foundation model.

## 7. Project recommendation for four implementation weeks

**Preferred direction: a compact glycan generator with explicit grammar constraints and a novelty/selection audit.** Use a restricted, fully specified subset from the GlyCosmosCCBY4export and report the exact filtering yield. Train a small autoregressive model; compare constrained decoding against a simple tree/Markov mutation baseline. Evaluate syntax, atom-level conversion coverage, structural-family holdout, exact and near-copy rates, and how much a frozen binding/property surrogate's score can be improved without moving into unsupported chemistry. The contribution should concern **reliable constrained design and evaluation**, not merely swapping an encoder or claiming the first glycan generator.

Functional conditioning should be a **second, conditional scope choice**: first obtain an immediately downloadable explicitly licensed array table, normalize/select a single assay context, and establish a credible family-held-out predictor. The GlyGen portal is a promising source, but its complete downloadable training table was not verified here. If that step fails, keep the project as structure generation/completion and assess controlled motif conditions; **do not relabel motif occurrence as measured lectin affinity**. Do not promise prospective binding validation without experimental collaboration.

| Implementation week | Concrete output |
|---|---|
|1|Freeze dataset snapshot, license/provenance and supported grammar; canonicalize/deduplicate before family split; benchmark setup,100–500trainingsteps and validity checks.|
|2|Train small unconditional/grammar baselines; measure actualT4seconds/update and peakVRAM; stop architectures whose extrapolated training+eval exceedsbudget.|
|3|Add motif/selectivitycondition only if a legitimate label/surrogate exists; compare unconstrained/constraineddecoding and simplemutation/search under equalcompute.|
|4|Finish family-held-out evaluation, near-neighbor/surrogate-exploitation analysis, ablations and reproducible notebook/report.|

**Proposed budget, E not a benchmark:** reserve1h for the basicgenerator,.75h for conditionaltraining,.5h for baseline/surrogate,.5h for sampling/evaluation and.5hbuffer, total **3.25GPUh**. Keep the model in the low-single-digit-million-parameter range, use preinstalledPyTorch and avoid a dependency stack requiring customCUDAcompilation. Enforce the five-minute fresh-environment requirement with a timed notebook before committing. If the first week's pilot does not pass, simplify the model/task; this audit does **not** certify the proposed budget.

If you choose NPs instead, a carefully versioned COCONUT/olderNPAtlas subset with a smallLSTM and structural-family novelty evaluation is easier than antibiotic discovery, but **unconditionalNPgeneration itself is crowded**. A microbial-only benchmark/provenance and stereochemistry audit could be useful; sparsely available activity labels and biosynthesis validation make a genuinely new antibioticinverse-design project a poor fit for these constraints.

## 8. Search coverage, unresolved evidence and reproducibility files

Searches combined glycan/carbohydrate/lectin with generative, inverse design, Markov, diffusion, transformer, masked completion and structure inference; NP/microbial/antibiotic/COCONUT/NPAtlas with language model, VAE, motif, GFlowNet and diffusion. Named project papers were followed to their official repositories, data statements and supplements; forward/review reference checks identified NPCLMs, SpaceGFN, EPOSMol and ShEPhERD. Search termination is the audit date, not the end of2026.

Boundary exclusions: **NaFM**, [Pretraining a foundation model for small-molecule natural products](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42256-026-01226-8), is a representation/prediction model in the primary description checked; it is distinct from generativeNPCLMs. **DiSE**, [arXiv2510.26231](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.26231), was identified as general spectrum-conditioned organic-molecule generation, but a specific microbial/NP dataset experiment was not verified, so it is an adjacent lead rather than an audited NP-model row. General all-atom models such as AlphaFold3/Boltz and general AMP generators are not counted as glycan-topology/NP generators. Related glycan encoders beyond the named baselines are not exhaustively catalogued. Historical2018[NP-templateRXRdesign](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42004-018-0068-1) is another reason not to claim NP-conditionedgeneration is new, but falls outside the requestedwindow.
