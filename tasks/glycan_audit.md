# Glycan work audit (primary-source notes)

Scope: this is a primary-source audit of the named glycan work and the structure-generative boundary. “Generation” here means a model emits a whole glycan/topology, completes a missing topology, or emits 3D coordinates; binding/property prediction and latent representation alone do not count as inverse design. Findings checked 2026-10-23. Exact model/data/train details are distinguished from unreported items.

## Direct answer on whether glycan generation exists

The generative side is **not empty**. GlycoBART (2025) is conditional sequence generation from an MS/MS spectrum into an IUPAC-like glycan sentence; it is intended to infer the glycan represented by that spectrum, and may output structures outside its training glycan labels. GlycanGT (2026) can fill masked monosaccharide/linkage tokens in a partly specified glycan, but this is masked-component completion, not demonstrated function-directed de novo design. SweetFold (2026 preprint) generates 3D conformations conditioned on a specified glycan sequence and optional protein context; it does not choose a new glycan topology to optimize a function. I found no 2022–2026 model in this named set that directly conditions on a target lectin/function or measured binding and outputs a new glycan topology optimized for binding. The clearest design gap is function-conditioned glycan topology generation with prospective experimental validation.

## Compute/reproducibility ranking (glycan subset only)

This ranking concerns *training plus evaluation on a free T4/P100 under 4 GPU-hours and setup under 5 min*, not inference with published weights. It also applies the immediate open-license/no-gate requirement.

1. **GlycanGT** — lowest computational barrier among the actual masked-completion/representation projects: 83.7k graphs and small/medium graph transformer variants make a reduced or fine-tune replication plausible within a short session, but authors’ complete training run was on L40 and no runtime/batch/epochs are reported. Full strict reproduction is **uncertain**, and public HF weights have a login/account-info gate. Setup requirement with Python 3.12 + Torch CUDA + Triton is **not verified <5 min**.
2. **GlycanAA (downstream only)** — property-head training on the benchmark can likely fit T4, but full self-supervised pretraining is **not reproducible under the constraint**: paper used 10×4090 for 50 epochs. Data license/bundle access also needs confirmation. Not a generative project.
3. **GlycoBART** — model size 207M; training reported on 4×A5000, 30 epochs, batch 80. No wall time. A T4 may fit with mixed precision/gradient accumulation but **under 4 GPU-hours is unsupported and likely unrealistic for faithful training**; 1.5GB XLSX ingestion/setup alone is friction. Dataset has no explicit license confirmed; fails strict data gate.
4. **SweetFold** — **not feasible** for training within the bound: H100 use and adaptation of Boltz-1x-scale diffusion. Its Colab notebook supports pretrained inference, not retraining in 4h. The paper’s Zenodo dataset license/file sizes were not confirmed in this audit.
5. **LectinOracle / SweetNet / glycowork** — useful as baseline predictors or validators, not generation. Their inference/fine-tuning may be T4-suitable, but this does not create a generative reproduction project. Full row-specific timings are not reported by the papers/repos checked.

## Addendum: training-source/config audit and corrections

These details come from opening the supplementary material references and actual public training files after the first pass. Repo defaults and notebook code are distinguished from paper-selected settings; where the current repository is mutable, preserve that distinction.

### GlycanGT actual training recipe and model scale

The article explicitly says batch size, epochs chosen by early stopping, and GPU-hours are not stated in its main text; the training-source audit narrows this. Paper method: four scales (ss/small/medium/large), 90/10 train/validation split, stop after 15 epochs without improvement for ss/small or 30 for medium/large, then retrain on the full data; AdamW lr 1e-6, wd 0.01; L40 46GB, Python 3.12/PyTorch 2.5.1 CUDA 12.1. The supplementary spreadsheet specifically lists **model dimensions/parameter counts** and validation losses/epochs; the OUP supplemental ZIP was linked but was not machine-readable/downloadable through the browser in this audit. Repo config supplies dimensions: ss 4 layers, 128 hidden, 512 FFN, 4 heads; small 6/256/768/8; medium 8/512/2048/16; large 12/768/3072/32, with 64-d orthogonal random node IDs. **The exact parameter counts remain unverified; do not present dimensions as a parameter count.**

The checked-in `training_node_and_edge/mask_35/pretrain_final_mask_35.py` is more concrete and differs from a generic README: full-set training uses batch 32 and pinned epoch counts ss 158, small 234, medium 506, large 485, sequentially trains all four, and sets AdamW 1e-6 / edge loss weight .5. Thus faithful full-repo run entails 1,383 total epochs across variants, 83,739 graphs and roughly 3.62 million minibatches at batch 32 (estimate from stated sample count; drop-last ignored). It uses one visible CUDA device in script, while paper says experiments on L40 GPUs. This current repo training recipe and paper’s validation-selected protocol are not identical in presentation; report both, don't silently choose one. **No elapsed time/GPU-hours reported.** A numeric wall-time estimate is weak without source epoch runtimes. At 83,739 samples and batch 32, there are about 2,617 batches/epoch; the large variant alone is 2,617 × 485 ≈ 1.27 million optimizer steps. For a 4-GPU-hour ceiling (14,400 seconds), large alone would require ≤11.3 ms/step on the target T4, before validation/evaluation; all four sizes require ≤4.0 ms/step. These are threshold calculations, not measured speed. Therefore full official-repo recipe is not a credible under-4h T4 reproduction; a single small model/shorter epochs is a separate reduced experiment, not a faithful reproduction. Sources: [paper methods/compute](https://academic.oup.com/bioinformatics/article/42/4/btag147/8551567), [training script](https://github.com/matsui-lab/GlycanGT/blob/main/training/training_node_and_edge/mask_35/pretrain_final_mask_35.py), [config](https://github.com/matsui-lab/GlycanGT/blob/main/model/config_tokengt.py), [source data folder](https://github.com/matsui-lab/GlycanGT/tree/main/data/clean). The repo’s data can be processed directly; GlyCosmos source has 244,842 entries at the June 2025 snapshot, processed paper count is 83,739. A separate explicit dataset license / file-byte size is not stated in paper/repo; code license is Apache-2.0.

### SweetNet correction and source-level details

Correct citation: Rebekka Burkholz, John Quackenbush, Daniel Bojar, “Using graph convolutional neural networks to learn a representation for glycans,” *Cell Reports* 35(11), 109251 (2021), DOI [10.1016/j.celrep.2021.109251](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.celrep.2021.109251), open access under CC BY 4.0. It predates the requested 2022–2026 window but is a named baseline. SweetNet is a predictor/embedding encoder, not generative. The paper’s “final SweetNet” specifies 128-dimensional node representations, three graph-convolution iterations, mean+max pooling, three dense layers; the current glycowork API specifies hidden_dim=128. The paper says batch 32, Adam, weight decay .001, initial lr .0005 cosine-decayed over 80 epochs, on a Tesla K80. GPU-hours/wall time are not reported. The paper’s virus binding variant uses **126,894 measured HA–glycan interactions** from 587 CFG glycan array screens and five independent runs; CFG array details are in the supplement. Repo is archived but MIT; CSV payload is `array_results.csv` 3,596,072 bytes, `glycan_recoding_master.csv` 162,796, `glyco_targets_species_seq_all_V3.csv` 2,217,171, `train_graph_full.csv` 10,531,324, `viral_meta.csv` 313,907 (all CSV, total 16,821,270 bytes). Checkpoint `sweetnet_kingdom.pt` is 2,149,731 bytes. Underlying dataset has source provenance/supplement but no separate data license was found; MIT is safely stated for repo code, not automatically all third-party array data. Paper metrics include classification accuracy and, for viral binding, MSE (0.7352 test); it has no output-structure novelty test. Paper/repo: [Cell Reports paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9208909/), [official archived repo](https://github.com/BojarLab/SweetNet).

**Training-time estimate (explicitly estimate, not reported):** for a taxonomy baseline over roughly 50k glycan sequences, batch 32 × 80 epochs is about 125k graph minibatches; the small three-layer graph network makes a single seed plausibly around 0.5–3 T4 GPU-hours assuming about 15–85 ms/batch, but this is an order-of-magnitude engineering estimate, not benchmarked. Five separate seeds could exceed 4 GPU-hours. The virus interaction model has 126,894 rows, giving about 317k batches over 80 epochs; at 15–85 ms per update this is **estimated** 1.3–7.5 T4 GPU-hours, so not safely within the cap. Include evaluation in addition; no source wall-clock exists.

### LectinOracle source-level details

Paper: Jon Lundstrøm, Emma Korhonen, Frédérique Lisacek, Daniel Bojar, *Advanced Science* 9(1), 2103807 (2022), DOI [10.1002/advs.202103807](https://doi.org/10.1002/advs.202103807). This is binding prediction, not structure generation. Paper confirms 3,228 CFG arrays plus 100 Imperial College arrays were curated; after requiring protein sequences and IUPAC mappings, model data contains 564,647 unique protein–glycan interactions according to the official repo. The split is 90/10 by protein (no protein overlap). Input combines ESM-1b sequence representation (1280d) and SweetNet glycan graph; learned glycan representation 256d and protein representation 128d are reported. Model training: one NVIDIA Tesla P100; batch 128; 100 max epochs, early stopping after 20; Adam lr .0005 with cosine decay over 80 epochs; MSE. Exact total trainable parameter count not stated. Repo notebook has a batch-128 trainer with patience 20 and 25-epoch default in a code path; paper-reported selected training is 100 max epochs, so cite the paper recipe for the publication and repo default only as implementation discrepancy. MIT repository contents/data: [official repo](https://github.com/BojarLab/LectinOracle), code and bundled files; measured file bytes from GitHub API: `all_arrays.csv` 12,762,021 bytes, `all_meta.csv` 1,245,254, background-correction CSV 169,575, motifs pickle 111,147, stored embeddings pickle 16,637,649, model checkpoint `LectinOracle_565.pt` 2,858,300; formats CSV, PKL, PT. These files are open-download without form/login, but no distinct license for the underlying CFG/Imperial data was located; repo MIT is code license, not proof of unrestricted re-use of third-party records. Paper metrics MSE/MAE on protein-held-out split, external-array correlation, qualitative literature validation. No generated-structure novelty question. Authors flag failure to capture all of DC-SIGN’s Lewis-X binding cluster, sensitivity to mutation interpretation as unresolved, and need for more diverse arrays. Sources: [primary paper search/full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8728848/), [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/advs.202103807), [official repo](https://github.com/BojarLab/LectinOracle). **GPU-hour estimate (estimate, not reported):** 564,647 / 128 × 100 ≈ 441,131 optimizer batches; for fixed ESM embeddings and modest graph/MLP network, at assumed 10–40 ms per batch, about 1.2–4.9 GPU-hours on a T4, excluding data prep/validation overhead. This range straddles the cap; reproducibility under four hours is borderline, and setup from older PyTorch-Geometric 1.8 stack could violate the five-minute environment constraint. The current repo checkpoint is much more practical than retraining.

### GlycoBART repository training files

Paper's full text (bioRxiv 2025) states GlycoBART: 4× NVIDIA RTX A5000, 30 epochs, batch 80; model 207M parameters, encoder/decoder 12 layers and 16 heads. Repo’s [`TrainGlycoBartFull.py`](https://github.com/cabsel/glycotrans/blob/master/training/glycobart/TrainGlycoBartFull.py) accepts layer counts, `d_model`, batch size, and epoch count as command-line inputs, uses AdamW lr 1e-5, DataLoader shuffle, DataParallel when multiple GPUs exist, and prints per-epoch wall-time. It does **not** hard-code a reproducible epoch/batch default and no training log/wall-time is published. Thus the article’s 4×A5000/30/80 are the authoritative reported values, and compute remains GPU-hours **not reported**. The article’s 1.5GB XLSX/807.9kB CSV Zenodo data record does not state a data license; repo code is MIT. No cross-check for near-duplicates beyond independent two-candidate novelty demonstration.

### GlycanML benchmark project (non-generative but relevant baseline/resource)

**GlycanML: A Multi-Task and Multi-Structure Benchmark for Glycan Machine Learning**, Minghao Xu, Yunteng Geng, Yihang Zhang, Ling Yang, Jian Tang, Wentao Zhang; Peking University/Mila/BioGeometry/HEC Montréal/CIFAR; ICLR 2025 conference paper, arXiv [2405.16206](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16206). It standardizes 11 property/function prediction tasks: 8 taxonomy levels, immunogenicity, glycosylation type, protein–glycan affinity. It provides graph and sequence representations, official splits, code, and leaderboard; no structure generation. The project page lists class counts (taxonomy: 4/11/39/101/210/415/922/1,737) and affinity is Z-transformed RFU; metrics include accuracy, AUPRC, Spearman rho. Official code [GlycanML/GlycanML](https://github.com/GlycanML/GlycanML) is Apache-2.0 and has run configs; install uses Python 3.8/3.9, PyTorch 1.12-era dependencies, TorchDrug/PyG, and ESM. Source-level accessible code is free, but README does not state a separate dataset license or bytes and (per GlycanGT repo) downstream datasets must be sourced from benchmark repo separately. No single model-level GPU hours: this is a benchmark, not one model; paper baselines are evaluated with configs and single-GPU instructions as well as multi-GPU. It is useful for evaluation and avoiding repeated benchmark collection, but not a generative project. Sources: [ICLR 2025 proceedings PDF](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/ca92ff06d973ece92cecc561757d500e-Paper-Conference.pdf), [official project page](https://glycanml.github.io/project/), [repo](https://github.com/GlycanML/GlycanML).

### Boltzmann-model glycan sequencing from lectin fingerprints (critical inverse-generation result)

**A Boltzmann model predicts glycan structures from lectin binding**, Aria Yom, Austin Chiang, Nathan E. Lewis; UC San Diego and Augusta University; peer-reviewed *Analytical Chemistry* 96(21), 8332–8341 (2024), DOI [10.1021/acs.analchem.3c04992](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.analchem.3c04992). The earlier preprint is bioRxiv 2023, DOI [10.1101/2023.06.03.543532](https://doi.org/10.1101/2023.06.03.543532). It is a bona fide **conditional structure-sequencing system**: from a measured multilectin/antibody binding fingerprint, it scores and ranks candidate glycan topologies. It does not optimize a newly generated glycan for one desired binding target; the conditioning signal is a vector of observed binding outcomes across a panel, and the objective is to infer the unknown input glycan. This is an important prior result and directly means binding-conditioned inverse glycan inference is not an empty area.

It combines a fully visible Boltzmann model (likelihood of motif presence given lectin-binding binaries) with a second-order Markov tree sampler: each next monosaccharide/linkage depends on the previous two residues. The authors generate on the order of 10^4 plausible CFG-like glycans to cover the modeled space, then rank these and measured CFG candidates under the binding likelihood. Training/evaluation data are CFG glycan microarray fingerprints; 64 lectins plus 9 antibodies, with an 80/20 random glycan split and results averaged across 64 random partitions. The article does not state the total number of usable glycans/fingerprint rows in the text opened, so that count is **not reported here** rather than inferred from external CFG catalog size. The article uses binding thresholds from 1,000 to 20,000 RFU to binarize each lectin–glycan interaction. Source is the [Consortium for Functional Glycomics (CFG)](https://www.functionalglycomics.org/); the paper’s [supplementary package](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10274649/) is 2.8 MB ZIP plus 2.1 MB PDF, but the page does not provide a clear explicit license for the underlying array measurements or a confirmed machine-readable raw-data bundle. Thus **free immediate use with an explicit open license is unconfirmed**. No application/login gate was identified for the article/supplement itself; original CFG dataset access conditions should be verified before reuse.

Training is gradient descent on a convex likelihood with step size 0.1, no regularization, and the authors state all training procedures converge to the same unique maximum-likelihood solution. Epoch count, batch size, hardware, and wall-time/GPU-hours are **not reported**. The model is small tabular/feature likelihood optimization and glycan sampling is a Markov process, so it should be easy to rerun on CPU; this is a qualitative assessment, not measured timing. No code repository or software license was located in the article/paper sources searched. Reported exact-structure accuracy is 61±10% for held-out N-glycans and top-3 accuracy 88±7%; O-glycan top-3 accuracy 87±13%, with problematic Sp14 presentation noted. CHO generalization was tested on 25 CHOGlycoNET glycans overlapping CFG. This is a structural recovery metric, not a prospective design assay. Leakage control is a random glycan split (80/20) and repeated partitions; no explicit near-duplicate or motif-neighbor audit is described, and random splitting may place closely related structures across partitions. Authors note the approximate nature of candidate generation, O-glycan array spacer artifact, and that one-residue Markov generation can produce pathological glycans; the second-order model was adopted to improve plausibility. Primary sources: [2024 final article in PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11162346/), [accessible preprint full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10274649/), [PubMed record and journal DOI](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38720429/).

This paper changes the gap statement above: a defensible gap is **prospective target-directed glycan topology design** (generate new glycan candidates to maximize one specified lectin or phenotype, ideally with validated experiments), not “no glycan inverse/generative model exists.” Existing work covers MS/MS-to-sequence generation (GlycoBART), partial topology completion (GlycanGT), multilectin-profile-to-structure sequencing (Boltzmann model), and 3D conformational generation for a supplied identity (SweetFold). None of these named systems demonstrates a scalable single-target glycan-design loop with experimental validation.
